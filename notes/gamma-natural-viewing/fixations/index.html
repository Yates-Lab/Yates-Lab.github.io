How to read the spectra and envelopes

Spike phase is the angle of the analytic signal after a zero-phase 4th-order Butterworth bandpass in the selected band. It is sampled at each original spike timestamp by interpolating complex voltage, not wrapped angles. Processing uses continuous context, with at least 250 ms withheld on either side of rejected samples, acquisition gaps and recording edges. The phase guide is a filtered waveform; filters can make a transient look cyclic. The envelope cutoff is a display choice in µV, not a calibrated detection threshold. A phase exists in filtered noise too. The spectrograms retain the separate taper/window settings above. These phases use unnotched LFP and no response-delay shift.

All spike colors use the selected LFP contact. Same-contact spike contamination and shared artifacts can contribute; a common color is not evidence for phase locking. Muller/Reynolds generalized phase addresses broader waveforms and is not the estimator used here. This display uses bandpass/Hilbert phase, not a measure of encoded information.

Time zero is saccade end. Dashed lines mark this fixation's boundaries, gray time is outside it. Green bars below the maps mark complete windows inside the fixation. Eye spectra add the separate horizontal and vertical powers of the selected signal: Peye = Phorizontal + Pvertical. We do not add the signed traces or compute a spectrum of their magnitude. Position uses the recorded positions directly; velocity uses their finite differences. Differentiation emphasizes faster variation, so their absolute spectra have different shapes and units. The same native-clock tapers, requested window duration and output time grid apply to both; position samples and midpoint velocity samples can shift the exact window edges by about half an eye sample. Maps and envelopes use full unnotched LFP, not the blue high-pass display trace. Windows can include nearby saccades; missing samples leave gaps. Envelope axes retain separate eye and LFP units. No latency shift or coupling test is applied.

White time is the selected fixation. Gray time is context outside it. Dashed boundaries mark saccade end and fixation end. The blue trace removes slow activity for viewing; it is unnotched and is not a gamma-burst detector.
